Title: What does the measurement establish?

[Read HTML](https://causality.in/collections/evidence-and-evaluation/)

Author: Harsh Joshi

This collection asks what a measurement establishes about a change, and why a candidate must not choose the test that admits it.

The decision to keep a record changes what an agent can later retrieve. Retrieval changes what it does, and what it does changes the outcomes available for scoring that record. Usefulness is entangled with the policy that gives a record its chance to be useful.

A faster request after a cache rollout raises a similar difficulty. The cache may have helped, or the traffic may have changed. A [comparison that separates those explanations](https://causality.in/collections/evidence-and-evaluation/from-observation-to-evaluation/) is more informative than the before-and-after number alone. In memory, the comparison becomes harder still: an early cue may matter only after many intervening decisions, and the record with the newest reward may not deserve the credit.

Estimating a contribution and deciding how much confidence to place in that estimate are different problems. Repeated runs can be stable while sharing one mistaken premise. Independent-looking reports can all descend from the same observation. Coverage can omit the very regime in which a rule fails. These distinctions affect the result itself, not only the presentation of an evaluation.

Small exact examples make some of the difficulty visible. [An influence can cancel along a path](https://causality.in/blog/when-effects-cancel/), and [a moving support can change a detection rate](https://causality.in/blog/when-the-support-moves/). Their assumptions differ, but both make a tempting shortcut answerable to a constructed case. The aim is a useful estimate whose interpretation survives closer examination, so that the next decision is informed by what was actually learned.
