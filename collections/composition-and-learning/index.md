Title: Composition and learning

[Read HTML](https://causality.in/collections/composition-and-learning/)

This collection is about how a computation's organisation, which parts interact and what they keep, can be learned rather than declared.

A reasoning architecture can contain more than a set of capable parts. Which parts interact, what experience they retain and how their roles change can themselves become learned computation. The attraction is an organisation shaped by the task: local specialisation, useful memory and information reaching the place where it changes an answer.

[MUON](https://causality.in/muon/) investigates the adaptive local unit; [HENA](https://causality.in/hena/) investigates the collective. Their ambition reaches beyond placing stock models in a fixed workflow. A hierarchy could arise through local interaction and evolve with what the system is trying to do. An early cue reaching a later request is one concrete demand on that organisation, not its whole purpose.

[Q-MUON](https://causality.in/q-muon/) and [Q-HENA](https://causality.in/q-hena/) ask the same two questions on a quantum substrate, so two essays here compare a quantum unit with its classical counterpart and ask what recovering a saved swarm can preserve once its resources have changed.

Messages still consume capacity, memory choices lose things and local improvements can conflict. Following the interaction into its consequences reveals whether the learned organisation helped: what relation survived, which route carried it, what the group could answer and what the full computation cost.
