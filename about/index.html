Can a law depend not only on a path’s past, but on where that path can go? Causality follows that question into the descriptions we use: what would let a system change the way it represents a problem, the questions it can ask and how it organises its computation?

A system can change its state, learn a skill or revise its declared world while keeping the language of that world fixed. Changing the language reaches another level: the same old description may now mean something different. Changing how the system draws distinctions reaches deeper still. Causality joins mathematical and architectural research across that ladder, asking what each change preserves and what it makes possible.

The calculus and its experiments

The mathematical centre

Pale holds a declared frame fixed while work proceeds inside it. MONAD asks what would let that frame change: what a description can tell apart, what it loses and what happens when it needs new questions or rules of change. Its proof library establishes particular cases: which answers a summary keeps, what another observation can recover and how a finite budget limits the next question. Those results give the larger calculus something exact to build on. Changing a rule without moving the finish line makes one boundary concrete: a system may improve its way of working without quietly changing what counts as success.

MONAD

One reasoning unit

MUON investigates one adaptive reasoning unit: how earlier input changes its next answer, what it remembers and what it learns. Stock models come first behind a common interface, as the baseline for comparison; a native micro-transformer unit is the long-term building block.

MUON

Many units

HENA investigates intelligence arising among units: learned sparse communication, limited distributed memory and emergent hierarchy. Organisation becomes something the architecture learns, rather than only an arrangement a designer supplies.

HENA

What would a learned architecture have to improve?

Pale gives people and agents an explicit account of their work: who is responsible, what each participant may do and which context a task needs. Its local compiler checks that declared account. HENA asks a further question: could useful organisation emerge and adapt through local interactions, so less of it has to be prescribed?

Counsel supplies local judgments within Pale’s organisation. MUON and HENA investigate those two scales as a learning architecture: the unit and the collective. HENA is Pale’s research successor: if its learned organisation proves useful, it could take over some of the coordination Pale describes explicitly. Pale, which sits around the coding agents and applications people already use, would then stand to it as a harness stands to the model it runs, more guardrail than organiser, still controlling permissions and external actions. HENA needs MUON’s unit and MONAD’s account of how a description can change, and the more the system organises itself, the more it depends on both. The mechanism remains open, and Pale’s own usefulness does not wait for it. In Harsh’s account HENA is an evolving swarm of MUONs that reasons about what could have happened, the counterfactual rung of the ACI framework in Beyond the Mirage. The products come first because they let agents see what a change would affect before they make it.

Keep the experience that helps. Explore the substrates that could carry it.

Reckon studies which retained experience helps later work. Its laboratory compares memory policies under a budget. Reckon’s design also preserves an experiment’s inputs for reuse, compares improvements to everyday tools as well as Causality research candidates, and keeps the decision an owner makes from the result. Running an evaluation and deciding to use its candidate remain separate acts. For everyday tools Reckon works from Pale’s history of what each piece of work relied on and what followed; without that history it has nothing to compare.

Q-MUON and Q-HENA explore the unit and collective architectures through quantum states, gates, channels and entanglement-mediated memory. Both classical and quantum paths are permanent peers. A different substrate opens research possibilities; its useful contribution has to be found, not assumed.

Planning over predictions, whole-trajectory constraints and equations with future states give the originating intuition different mathematical forms. The endpoint example follows what information each form requires.
